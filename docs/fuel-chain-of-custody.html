Operations · Loss Prevention

Fuel Chain of Custody

A filling station only stays honest when every litre is independently verifiable at every handoff — from the depot's meter to the pump's nozzle. This is the modern flow, checkpoint by checkpoint, and the control that makes each one hold.

The Principle

Three records that must agree

No single log can be trusted on its own — theft hides in the gap between what was supposedly sent, what arrived, and what was sold. Reconcile three independently generated numbers, and a mismatch tells you exactly where the chain broke.

01 · Source
Dispatched
The depot's own calibrated meter, sent electronically to HQ the moment the tanker leaves — never carried only on paper by the driver.
02 · Forecourt
Received
The station's seal check and dip reading the moment the tanker arrives, taken before a single drop is offloaded.
03 · Ledger
Accounted For
The daily tank dip plus every pump's sealed meter total, reconciled against what was actually sold.

Do the three tie out?

The Flow

Seven checkpoints, one unbroken chain

Fuel physically passes through these stages in order. Each one produces its own record, so a loss can be traced to the exact link where it happened.

01

Depot Dispatch

HappensThe tanker loads at the source depot against your purchase order; the depot's calibrated meter records exact litres per compartment.
ControlCompartments are sealed immediately with uniquely numbered, tamper-evident seals before the tanker leaves the gate.
RecordA digital dispatch note — product, quantity per compartment, seal numbers, driver, tanker registration — sent straight to HQ and the receiving station, independent of the driver.
02

Transit

HappensThe tanker travels the approved route to the station.
ControlGPS with geofencing flags any route deviation or unscheduled stop; electronic seals, where fitted, transmit an "opened" event with time and location if broken en route.
RecordA live, timestamped route log attached to the delivery.
03

Arrival & Seal Verification

HappensOn arrival, station staff check every seal number against the digital dispatch note — before anything is offloaded.
ControlA broken, missing, or mismatched seal stops the delivery on the spot and escalates immediately. Nobody offloads first and "sorts it out after."
RecordA seal-verification entry with photos, logged against the dispatch note.
04

Offload & Dip Reconciliation

HappensAn opening dip is taken on the receiving tank, the tanker offloads, then a closing dip is taken.
ControlThe tank's rise must match the dispatched quantity within normal thermal-expansion tolerance — roughly 0.3–0.5% of volume. The person confirming this is never the person who raised the purchase order.
RecordBefore/after dip readings, variance, and sign-off — tied to the dispatch note and seal check.
05

Daily Tank Reconciliation

HappensEvery day: opening stock + deliveries − metered pump sales is checked against the tank's actual closing dip.
ControlAutomatic Tank Gauges (ATG), where fitted, give a continuous electronic cross-check against the manual dip rather than replacing it.
RecordA daily variance figure per tank, auto-flagged the moment it crosses threshold.
06

Pump Shift Controls

HappensEach pump's sealed, non-resettable totalizer is read at shift open and shift close; litres dispensed is the difference.
ControlCash and mobile-money collected must match litres dispensed × price. Attendants never fuel their own vehicle off-log, and shift/pump assignments rotate unpredictably.
RecordA shift reconciliation: opening/closing meter readings, collections, and variance.
07

Escalation & Investigation

HappensAny variance past its threshold — at delivery, in the daily tank figure, or on a shift — is routed for review, never quietly written off.
ControlRepeat variances tied to the same attendant, pump, or tanker are tracked as a pattern, not filed as separate one-off incidents.
RecordAn approval/investigation entry with the underlying reconciliation attached as evidence.

People

No one person touches the whole chain

Technology closes the gaps a determined person can't talk their way around; segregation of duties closes the ones a camera can't see.

—

Order, receive, and reconcile are three different people. Whoever places the fuel order never receives it, and never reconciles the books against it.

—

Rotation breaks patterns. Attendants move across pumps and shifts unpredictably, so collusion has no stable pairing to settle into.

—

Spot checks stay unannounced. A manager or auditor dips a tank outside the normal schedule, at random.

—

Reporting doesn't route through a supervisor. Staff can flag suspected theft through a channel that bypasses the person who might be involved.

Technology

What actually closes the gaps

None of this replaces the people controls above — it removes the manual steps where a number could otherwise be quietly changed.

Automatic Tank Gauges
Continuous electronic level, temperature, and water-in-fuel readings, cross-checked against the manual dip rather than replacing it.
GPS & Geofencing
Route and stop monitoring on every tanker, independent of the driver's own account of the trip.
Tamper-Evident Seals
Uniquely numbered; electronic variants transmit an "opened" event the instant they're broken, with time and location.
POS-Integrated Meters
Litres sold come straight from the pump's sealed totalizer into the sale record — never typed in by hand.
Timestamped CCTV
Forecourt and tank-farm footage indexed to transaction times, not just left as a general recording.
Centralized Reconciliation
Every station's dips, deliveries, and variances visible from one place in real time, instead of paper reports arriving days later.
